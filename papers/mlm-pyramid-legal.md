# MLM, pyramid schemes and legal boundary

Where multilevel compensation crosses into pyramid territory. Not legal advice; the point is which design features regulators and courts look at.

**Category takeaway:** the line is about **where the money comes from** (sales of real products/services to real end users vs. recruitment and participant purchases), not about tree depth. A depth cap of 10 is engineering and optics, not a safe harbor.

---

### Marketing Fraud: An Approach for Differentiating Multilevel Marketing from Pyramid Schemes
- **Citation:** P. J. Vander Nat, W. W. Keep. Journal of Public Policy & Marketing 21(1), 2002, pp. 139–151.
- **Link:** <https://doi.org/10.1509/jppm.21.1.139.17603> (verified via Crossref)
- **Summary:** Offers an analytical approach for telling legitimate MLM from pyramid schemes, focusing on whether compensation is driven by retail sales to ultimate users or by recruitment and internal (participant) consumption. Models how recruitment-driven rewards imply that most participants must lose.
- **Implication for referralTree:** Every `rewardRoots` should be funded by real external value (e.g., contest fees, product revenue), never by new-member buy-ins. LightForest project records (the published terms) should disclose the funding source of each group.

### Multilevel Marketing and Pyramid Schemes in the United States: An Historical Analysis
- **Citation:** W. W. Keep, P. J. Vander Nat. Journal of Historical Research in Marketing 6(2), 2014.
- **Link:** <https://doi.org/10.1108/jhrm-01-2014-0002> (verified via Crossref)
- **Summary:** Traces the history of MLM and pyramid enforcement in the US (including FTC cases) and how the legal tests evolved around retail sales and recruitment rewards.
- **Implication for referralTree:** Positioning: "affiliate attribution with multilevel share, funded by app revenue." Avoid matrix/forced-fill mechanics and buy-in requirements; get counsel before any group where participants pay to join.

### FTC and State of Washington v. Amway Corp. et al. (W.D. Wash. No. 2:26-cv-03474), stipulated order
- **Citation:** Stipulated Order for Permanent Injunction, Monetary Judgment, and Other Relief, *FTC & State of Washington v. Amway Corp., World Wide Group, L.L.C., Leadership Team Development, Inc.*, No. 2:26-cv-3474 (W.D. Wash.), filed 17 Sep 2026; entered 13 Oct 2026 per Npros (docket entry not independently checked).
- **Link:** order as filed: <https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Amway-StipulatedOrder.pdf> (verified, PDF read); entry and payment schedule: <https://npros.com/news/judge-enters-amway-s-225-million-ftc-settlement-starting-clock-on-payments-and-ibo-notices/> (trade press)
- **Summary:** $225M total ($154.7M Amway, $39.78M WWG, $26.52M LTD, $4M to Washington), the largest FTC recovery from an MLM; a settlement, not a pyramid-scheme finding. Section I.A of the order limits pay to personal and downline sales to eligible customers, requires each participant's eligible customer sales to be ≥70% of monthly product volume, and when a participant falls short, cuts both that participant's credited volume (Appendix A formula) and, after the participant's first six months, **every upline's credit from that participant by the same proportion**. Uplines get no credit for a recruit's unsold purchases in the recruit's first six full months. Under I.B, an upline may not enroll anyone until it has made eligible customer sales to at least three different customers and finished training. Per Npros, the comp-plan rules start nine months after entry and run ten years.
- **Implication for referralTree:** The newest US template for "where the money comes from" is concrete: upline credit flows only from real external sales and is cut pro rata, all the way up, when a downline's volume isn't real. That is a regulator-written version of the decayed ancestor penalty (it scales upline pay down rather than slashing) and of gating who may refer (three real customers before enrolling anyone). Point to it when explaining why referralTree pays only on oracle-verified external outcomes, and consider "has produced N real outcomes" as the rule for when a code can start taking children.
