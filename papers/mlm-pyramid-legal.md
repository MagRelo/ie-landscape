# MLM, pyramid schemes and legal boundary

Where multilevel compensation crosses into pyramid territory. Not legal advice; the point is which design features regulators and courts look at.

**Category takeaway:** the line is about **where the money comes from** (sales of real products/services to real end users vs. recruitment and participant purchases), not about tree depth. A depth cap of 10 is engineering and optics, not a safe harbor.

---

### Marketing Fraud: An Approach for Differentiating Multilevel Marketing from Pyramid Schemes
- **Citation:** P. J. Vander Nat, W. W. Keep. Journal of Public Policy & Marketing 21(1), 2002, pp. 139–151.
- **Link:** <https://doi.org/10.1509/jppm.21.1.139.17603> (verified via Crossref)
- **Summary:** Offers an analytical approach for telling legitimate MLM from pyramid schemes, focusing on whether compensation is driven by retail sales to ultimate users or by recruitment and internal (participant) consumption. Models how recruitment-driven rewards imply that most participants must lose.
- **Implication for referralTree:** Every `rewardRoots` should be funded by real external value (e.g., contest fees, product revenue), never by new-member buy-ins. Incentive Exchange listings should disclose the funding source of each group.

### Multilevel Marketing and Pyramid Schemes in the United States: An Historical Analysis
- **Citation:** W. W. Keep, P. J. Vander Nat. Journal of Historical Research in Marketing 6(2), 2014.
- **Link:** <https://doi.org/10.1108/jhrm-01-2014-0002> (verified via Crossref)
- **Summary:** Traces the history of MLM and pyramid enforcement in the US (including FTC cases) and how the legal tests evolved around retail sales and recruitment rewards.
- **Implication for referralTree:** Positioning: "affiliate attribution with multilevel share, funded by app revenue." Avoid matrix/forced-fill mechanics and buy-in requirements; get counsel before any group where participants pay to join.
